Part 01 — The Research

We studied the brands that
own colour at scale.

Each of these companies solved the same problem goSTOPS faces: how to use multiple colours without losing coherence. Their solutions shaped our framework.

Google
Four colours, one per product family. Blue = default. Red = YouTube. Green = Sheets. Yellow = Keep. The colour tells you which product you're in — silently.
Lesson: Colour as product wayfinding
Slack
Aubergine (#4A154B) is the constant — always present, always the base. Four accent colours dance on top but never replace it. One anchor rules all.
Lesson: One constant across every surface
NBC
Six feather colours, each mapped to a division. The full peacock only appears at network level. Sub-brands carry one colour. Sound familiar?
Lesson: Full palette = parent brand only
Microsoft
Window pane = four colours. Inside each product, one colour dominates. Teams is purple. Excel is green. Word is blue. Marketing never steals product colour.
Lesson: Marketing ≠ Product colour
Figma
Five colours but most surfaces show one or two at a time. Restraint is the secret — a controlled palette feels richer than an oversaturated one.
Lesson: Restraint makes colour pop
Olympics
Five rings — always together, never separated. The full palette is the mark itself. Individual events borrow one colour as an accent, never the full set.
Lesson: The mark is sacred
Part 02 — Six Principles

The rules that protect
the goSTOPS identity.

Distilled from the six brands above. These principles are non-negotiable — they apply to every surface, every team, every decision.

01
The Mark is Sacred
The full six-colour hand only appears as the logo. Never decompose it into parts. Never recolour it. The hand = goSTOPS. Period.
02
One Anchor Rules All
Like Slack's aubergine, goSTOPS has the Finger Stripe — the six-colour bar that appears on every surface. It's the brand's constant.
03
Colour Gets Assigned
Each colour maps to one business vertical. Pink = Stays + goLIVING (all accommodation). Purple = Events. Sky = Workation. Orange = goCOINS. Teal = Experiences. Yellow = Universal CTA.
04
Marketing ≠ Product
A social post about Workation can use Sky. But the product UI's primary CTA stays Yellow — always. Marketing colours shift; product colour is permanent.
05
Restraint Makes Colour Pop
One colour per surface. Two maximum. White space is sacred. The fewer colours on a surface, the more each one means.
06
One Constant Everywhere
The Finger Stripe appears on every surface — digital and physical. It's the thread that connects a room key to an Instagram post to a wall mural.
Part 03 — The Three-Layer Framework

The architecture that
holds everything together.

Three layers. Increasing freedom. Layer 1 is locked. Layer 2 is assigned. Layer 3 is earned. This hierarchy is how goSTOPS can use six colours without chaos.

Layer 1 — The Mark
Sacred. Untouchable.
The full six-colour logo + the Finger Stripe. These appear everywhere but are never modified, recoloured, or partially used.
Always in this order: Pink → Purple → Sky → Orange → Teal → Yellow
Layer 2 — The Vertical
Assigned. Flat. Clean.
Each business vertical gets one colour. Used as solid flat colour — on cards, badges, section accents, wayfinding. One colour per surface.
StaysCore properties
Events + MembersOpen mic, goINSIDER
WorkationRemote work, offsites
goCOINSRewards, cashback, loyalty
ExperiencesTreks, goLOCAL
YellowUniversal CTA — all verticals
Layer 3 — The Digital Lift
Earned. Not default.
On digital surfaces only, flat colour can activate into a gradient. Physical stays flat. The gradient is the earned elevation — a reward, not the baseline.
Pink → Purple · Booking confirmation hero
Sky → Teal · Workation ambient header
Orange → Yellow · goCOINS celebration
Rule: Gradient only on digital. Only as celebration or emphasis. Never on print, merch, or walls.
The logo is the mark. The flat colour is the vertical.
The gradient is the digital lift.
Three layers. One system. Every surface.

Part 05 — The Swirl

The hand carries the brand.
The swirl carries the soul.

The spiral at the palm of the goSTOPS hand isn't decoration — it's the DNA of movement, of journeys that loop back, of travellers who return. We extract it and weave it across every touchpoint as the brand's texture.

Source
The goSTOPS hand — the spiral lives at the base of the palm
Extract
The spiral extracted — a continuous curve that loops and returns
Apply
Corner
Watermark
Loader
Divider
Four uses: corner accent, watermark, loader, section divider
Swirl Usage Rules
DO
Use at 4–8% opacity as a background texture on cards and sections. Use as a corner accent (bottom-right) on property cards and post templates. Use the outline version as a section divider. Use the full hand as a watermark on merchandise and physical surfaces. Match swirl colour to the vertical colour of the surface.
DON'T
Never use the swirl as a primary graphic element — it's texture, not content. Never use it above 15% opacity — it's always subtle. Never use it as a replacement for the logo. Never mix swirl colours on a single surface. Never animate the swirl in a way that distracts from content.

Website Colour Theory — Rules
Navigation
Active tab text turns its vertical colour. Underline in vertical colour (2px solid). Yellow CTA button (BOOK NOW) stays permanently in the nav — never changes colour. The Finger Stripe sits directly below the nav bar as a constant.
Hero Sections
Homepage hero: Off-white canvas, photography, Pink accent. Vertical landing hero: Full vertical colour takeover — the entire hero background becomes the assigned colour. Dark text (#09090F) on light colours. White text on Purple/Pink.
Cards & Content
White cards on off-white canvas. Vertical colour appears as: left-border accent (4px), badge/pill, or category tag. Never as full card background. Property cards always use Yellow CTA for booking. The swirl appears as a subtle watermark (6% opacity) on hero images.
Footer
Dark canvas (#09090F). Finger Stripe at top of footer section. White text. goSTOPS logo (full colour). Hand swirl watermark at low opacity in the background. All six verticals listed with their dot colours for navigation.
